## Financing

iTop Pools is not a lender. Most homeowners finance at least part of a pool, usually with one of four options:
1. Unsecured pool loan: a personal loan for home projects. Fast approval, no home equity needed, but higher rates and shorter terms.
2. Home equity loan: a fixed amount borrowed against home equity. Lower fixed rates and longer terms, but the home is collateral and there are closing costs.
3. HELOC: a credit line drawn as the build progresses. Interest only on what is used, but variable rates and the home is collateral.
4. Cash-out refinance: refinance the mortgage for more than is owed. One payment and potentially the lowest rate, but it resets the mortgage.

Example only, not a loan offer: a $65,000 project financed for 15 years at 7.99% APR is about $621 a month. Rates and terms depend on the lender and the visitor's credit. iTop goes through options when presenting the price. Calculator: https://itoppools.com/pool-financing/

## Permits, HOAs and Florida pool rules

Every inground pool in Northeast Florida needs a building permit from the city or county you live in, and most neighborhoods also need HOA approval. Florida law requires a new residential pool to have at least one approved safety feature, such as a compliant barrier, a safety cover, door alarms or a certified pool alarm, before it passes final inspection. iTop prepares the drawings and handles both approvals.

- Who issues the permit: City of Jacksonville Building Inspection Division for most of Duval County (online via JaxEPICS); Jacksonville Beach, Atlantic Beach and Neptune Beach each have their own building department; St. Johns County Building Department for unincorporated St. Johns County (Ponte Vedra Beach, St. Johns, Julington Creek, most of Nocatee); Clay County Building Division for unincorporated Clay County (Fleming Island, Middleburg). The City of St. Augustine, St. Augustine Beach and Orange Park have their own building departments.
- Florida law (Chapter 515) requires a new residential pool to have at least one safety feature to pass final inspection: a compliant pool barrier, an approved safety cover, exit alarms on doors and windows to the pool, self-closing self-latching doors, or an ASTM F2208 pool alarm.
- A built-in spa or hot tub deeper than 24 inches counts as a pool under Florida law.
- iTop prepares the drawings, submits them to the HOA and the county, and tracks every approval. The homeowner doesn't fill out forms or make calls.

### Hurricane prep for your pool

Before a hurricane, leave your fiberglass pool full, because high groundwater after heavy rain can push an empty shell upward. Turn off equipment power and secure loose items. Don't restart equipment until it's dry and debris is cleared, and call iTop Pools if anything looks off after the storm.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why fiberglass

Q: Why choose a fiberglass pool over concrete or vinyl liner?
A: Fiberglass is the fastest of the three to install, needs the least upkeep and rarely carries a big future repair bill, which is why many Jacksonville families choose it. The one-piece shell is made in a factory and set in weeks rather than months. Its smooth, non-porous gelcoat gives algae less to grip, so it typically needs fewer chemicals and less brushing. Concrete is the better answer when you need a truly custom shape, and vinyl usually has the lowest upfront price. See the full comparison guide.

### Fiberglass vs concrete vs vinyl

Q: Which pool type costs least to own over 25 years?
A: Fiberglass usually has the lowest long-term ownership cost, because it avoids the two largest recurring bills: concrete resurfacing and vinyl liner replacement. Vinyl liners commonly need replacing every 10–15 years at about $3,000–$7,500 each, and Florida's sun can shorten that. Concrete needs periodic resurfacing and generally more chemicals. Actual costs depend on the pool and how it is cared for.

Q: When is concrete a better choice than fiberglass?

Q: Is vinyl cheaper than fiberglass?
A: Vinyl usually costs less upfront: Barrier Reef puts fiberglass at roughly 15–20% more than vinyl. But vinyl liners wear out and commonly need replacing every 10–15 years at about $3,000–$7,500 each, so the gap narrows over time. Compare both over the years you plan to own the pool.

### Florida-specific

Q: What pool safety rules apply in Florida?
A: Florida's Residential Swimming Pool Safety Act (Chapter 515, Florida Statutes) requires a new residential pool to have at least one approved safety feature, such as a compliant barrier, an approved safety cover, exit alarms or self-closing, self-latching doors. iTop Pools builds this into your quote. Q: Should I drain my pool before a hurricane?
